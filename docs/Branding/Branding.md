Product Naming Philosophy

The application should feel:

* Friendly
* Approachable
* Enjoyable
* Built for learning
* Memorable

Avoid names that feel like engineering utilities or generic training software.

The goal is to create something people look forward to using every day.

Candidate: Didah ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Meaning

“Didah” comes from the spoken cadence used while learning Morse code.

Rather than visualizing dots and dashes, operators learn to hear the rhythm of each character.

Examples:

Character	Spoken
A	didah
S	dididit
W	didahdah
5	dididididit

This aligns closely with the Long Island CW Club (LICW) teaching philosophy.

Why it works

* Authentic to Morse code.
* Recognizable to experienced operators.
* Educational.
* Short and memorable.
* Represents learning by sound instead of memorizing dots and dashes.
* Not limited to being “just a trainer.”

Possible Tagline

Learn the sound. Not the dots.

⸻

Candidate: Dit Dit ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Meaning

“Dit dit” is commonly used by many CW operators as an informal closing at the end of a QSO.

It roughly conveys:

* Thanks
* That’s all
* See you next time
* I’m done

It gives experienced operators a subtle “inside joke” while remaining friendly to beginners.

Why it works

* Warm and approachable.
* Playful.
* Memorable.
* Easy to pronounce.
* Easy to brand.
* Feels like something users will enjoy opening every day.

Branding Test

This passes the “say it out loud” test.

“I’m going to spend 15 minutes on Dit Dit tonight.”

That feels natural.
